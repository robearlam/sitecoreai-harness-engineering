## Title

Call to Action Banner

---

## Feature Overview

A full-width, authored banner that asks visitors to take one clear action, such as "Get in touch" or "Start your trial". It shows a heading, optional short body text, a primary action, and an optional secondary action. It sits on one of three fixed background colours, or on an optional background image behind a black scrim. It is placed in `headless-main` and gets its content from a shared datasource item. The approved specification (`harness/artifacts/specs/T007-feature-spec.md`) sets the scope. This note decides the open design questions it handed on: scrim opacity, button-stacking breakpoint, focus treatment for each background, and editing affordances for empty fields.

---

## Design Intent

- **One clear prompt.** The banner is a short, focused interruption in the page flow: a heading, a sentence or two, and the actions. Typography and spacing set the hierarchy. There is no decoration (no shadows, gradients, borders, or icons).
- **Fixed, binding palette.** Colours and button styles follow the human decision recorded in the task file and spec. No new colour tokens are introduced and no `primary` utilities are used. All colours are Tailwind default-palette classes, as existing components already use.
- **Distinct from the Hero Banner.** The CTA banner's height follows its content (no fixed or viewport height). Its content is centred, it uses an `<h2>`, and it is led by its actions. Its default look is a calm neutral band, not imagery. It does not share structure or code with the Hero Banner.
- **Predictable in any composition.** The banner reads as its own page section. It is separated from surrounding authored content by its background band, not by extra rules or margins, so it fits anywhere in `headless-main`.
- **Readable on every background.** Each background mode has a fixed text colour and button treatment that meets WCAG AA. Authors cannot produce an unreadable combination.

## Page / Feature Structure

The banner is one self-contained page section with three nested layers:

1. **Background band.** Spans the full viewport width. It shows either the selected colour, or the background image covered by the black scrim.
2. **Content container.** Centred inside the band and constrained to the application's existing content width. It has horizontal padding so content never touches the viewport edges.
3. **Content stack.** Centred text, in this order:
   - heading region (`<h2>`)
   - body region (optional)
   - action group (primary, then optional secondary)

The section is labelled by its heading for assistive technology, so it appears as a named region.

In editing mode only, the content stack also includes the editing affordances described under Editing Mode below.

## Section Breakdown

### Background band

- **Purpose:** Sets the banner apart as a distinct band in the page.
- **Colour modes** (binding):

  | Mode | Background | Text |
  |---|---|---|
  | Neutral Light (default, also used when no option is selected) | `neutral-100` | `neutral-900` |
  | Primary | `blue-700` | `white` |
  | Neutral Dark | `neutral-900` | `white` |
  | Background image | image + black scrim | `white` |

- **Image mode:** When an image is set, it replaces the colour whatever option is selected. The image covers the whole band, is cropped to fill it, and is centred. It is not stretched and never sets the banner's height, which still follows the content.
- **Scrim opacity (decision): black at 60% (`bg-black/60`), fixed.** Browsers blend the scrim in sRGB. In the worst case, a pure-white image under a 60% black scrim gives an effective background of about `#666666`. White text on that is about 5.7:1, which meets AA for normal text (4.5:1) with some margin. The minimum opacity that passes is about 54%, so 60% leaves headroom. Authors cannot change the scrim. Opacity options are out of scope.
- **Image semantics:** The background image is decorative and is hidden from assistive technology. The heading and body carry the meaning.

### Heading region

- An `<h2>` in the band's text colour, at the design-system H2 scale: 30px on mobile, 36px from tablet up (`text-3xl` to `text-4xl`). Weight is semibold or bold, to match existing headings. The line length is constrained so long headings wrap neatly.

### Body region

- Short-form rich text at body size (18px, `text-lg`), with generous line height. Body lines are capped at a readable width, narrower than the container (about `max-w-2xl`) and centred.
- The body uses the same colour as the band's text, not a muted tone, so contrast stays at the accepted pair values on every background. Reduced-opacity text is not used on any background.
- Links inside the body inherit the text colour and are underlined, so they don't rely on colour alone.
- When the body is empty, nothing is output in normal mode, and the spacing closes up so the heading leads straight into the actions.

### Action group

- Primary first, then secondary. Both are links styled as buttons, and their visible text comes from the authored link text.
- **Button styles (binding):**
  - On Primary, Neutral Dark, and image: primary = `white` fill with a `neutral-900` label; secondary = `white` outline (2px) with a `white` label and transparent fill.
  - On Neutral Light: primary = `neutral-900` fill with a `white` label; secondary = `neutral-900` outline (2px) with a `neutral-900` label and transparent fill.
- **Shape and size:** Both buttons share one size, padding, and corner radius, matching the minimal button style already used in the app. Both have a touch target of at least 44px tall. The two buttons differ only in fill versus outline.
- **Hover (subtle, as the design system asks):** Filled buttons shift slightly (white to `neutral-100`; `neutral-900` to `neutral-700`). Outline buttons get a faint tint of their outline colour (about 10% opacity). Every hover state keeps the label contrast at AA. There is no movement or scaling, only a short colour transition.
- **Focus treatment (decision):** A clearly visible ring on keyboard focus only (`focus-visible`). It is 2px wide with a 2px offset, so it sits outside the button and is never mistaken for the secondary button's outline.
  - Primary, Neutral Dark, image: `white` ring.
  - Neutral Light: `neutral-900` ring.
  - On all four backgrounds, the ring contrasts with the surrounding background at well above 3:1.
- When the secondary action is empty, it is omitted in normal mode and the primary button sits alone, centred. When the primary is empty in normal mode, it is also omitted. If both are empty, no action group is output.

### Editing mode (Pages / Experience Editor)

- **Heading, body, primary action, secondary action:** Each field stays in its normal position and is editable inline. Empty fields show the editor's standard empty-field placeholder in place, at roughly the size of the content they stand in for. The action group therefore always shows two slots in editing mode, primary then secondary, even when both are empty.
- **Background image:** A background image cannot be clicked, so editing mode shows a small, labelled "Background image" slot. It sits below the action group, inside the content container, and is visible whether the image is empty or set. When the image is set, the slot shows a thumbnail and the band also shows the image with its scrim, as the visitor will see it. This slot exists in editing mode only.
- **Background colour:** A Droplink can't be edited inline. Authors change it through the datasource item's fields in Pages. The field's help text says the image overrides the colour. The band updates to show the current selection.
- Editing affordances must stay readable on every background, including white text on dark bands. They are never output in normal mode.

## Component Recommendations

- **One banner component** with the three layers above. Background mode is chosen from the datasource. There are no rendering parameters or variants.
- **Reuse the existing content-container pattern** for width and horizontal padding, so the banner's content lines up with other `headless-main` sections.
- **Use the SDK's field primitives** for text, rich text, link, and image, so inline editing and empty-field placeholders behave the same as in other components.
- **Button presentation** is banner-level: two roles (primary and secondary), each with two background contexts (light and dark). Image mode uses the dark context. This is not a new design-system button pattern, per the binding decision.
- **The background-mode mapping** (option to background, text, button context, focus ring colour) should be a single lookup with Neutral Light as the fallback. Then every element on the banner draws from the same source.
- Do not reuse or couple to the Hero Banner's structure or styling.

## Responsive Behaviour

### Mobile (below 640px, below `sm`)

- The band has 48px of vertical padding (`py-12`) and at least 16px of horizontal padding.
- The heading is 30px and the body 18px, both centred.
- **Buttons stack vertically**, primary on top, with 12–16px between them. Each button fills the container width (capped at a comfortable maximum), so tap targets are large and the labels line up.
- Long link text wraps inside the button and never overflows horizontally.

### Tablet (640px and up, `sm` to below `lg`)

- **Buttons sit side by side** from `sm` up. They are centred, sized to their content, and 16px apart. If the labels are too long to fit side by side, the buttons wrap onto a second line rather than overflowing.
- The band's vertical padding rises to 64px (`py-16`) from `md`. The heading grows to 36px from `md`.

### Desktop (`lg` and up)

- The layout matches tablet. The band keeps spanning the viewport while the content stays at the container width, and the body stays at its capped reading width. Nothing gets wider on large screens apart from the band.

### Cross-breakpoint rules

- No horizontal overflow at any width, including with long headings, link text, or wide images.
- The order is always heading, body, primary, secondary.
- The image always covers the band at every size. The band's height follows the content, never the image's aspect ratio.

## Accessibility Considerations

- **Semantics:** The banner is a section labelled by its `<h2>`. It never outputs an `<h1>`. Actions are real links (role `link`) with discernible text from the authored link text. No icons are used.
- **Contrast:** The colour pairs are accepted as AA in the spec. The image case is covered by the 60% scrim (about 5.7:1 in the worst case with a white image). Button labels, outlines (white or `neutral-900` against their bands), and focus rings all meet the relevant AA thresholds on their backgrounds. Hover states keep AA.
- **Keyboard:** Both actions can be reached in reading order (primary, then secondary). The focus ring appears for keyboard users on every background, as described above.
- **Decorative image:** The background image is hidden from assistive technology. Any meaning has to live in the heading and body.
- **Motion:** The only motion is short colour transitions on hover. There is no animation.
- **Link text:** Authors should write action labels that make sense on their own ("Get in touch", not "Click here"). This belongs in template help text, not in component logic.

## Risks

- **Background image editability.** If the editing-only image slot doesn't render, or can't be selected, the author can't reach the image field in Pages. This is the failure the task file warns about.
- **Editing chrome on dark bands.** The editor's empty-field placeholders may be hard to read on `blue-700`, `neutral-900`, or image bands. This needs a manual check in Pages on each background.
- **Decorative image versus authored alt text.** Treating the image as decorative means any alt text the author enters is not announced. This is right for a background, but authors may expect otherwise.
- **Very long authored content.** Long headings or link labels make the banner tall and wrap the buttons. Layout copes with this, but long content weakens the banner's purpose. The template help text should encourage brevity.
- **Existing button and container conventions.** This note assumes the app already has a content-container width and a minimal button shape to reuse. If it doesn't, planning should choose values on the design-system spacing scale, not invent a new pattern.
